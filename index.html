Pharyngeal arches and pouches

The arches build the jaw, ear bones, hyoid and larynx. The pouches build the middle ear, tonsil, thymus and parathyroid glands.

By the end you can

  • Match each arch to its nerve, muscles and skeleton.
  • Name what each of the four pouches becomes.
  • Explain DiGeorge syndrome as failure of the 3rd and 4th pouches.

Theory

What the arches are

In weeks 4 and 5, a row of swellings appears on each side of the future neck. These are the pharyngeal (branchial) arches. Every arch has the same five parts: a core of mesenchyme from neural crest cells, a cartilage rod, an aortic arch artery, a cranial nerve and a muscle mass.

Humans have five arches, numbered 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6. The 5th never forms as a separate arch. Between the arches there are four grooves on the outside and four pouches on the inside. A thin membrane separates each groove from the pouch behind it.

The rule that solves most questions Muscle follows nerve. A muscle that forms in an arch keeps that arch's nerve, wherever it ends up in the adult.

Key takeaways

  • Arch nerves in order: V, VII, IX, X (superior laryngeal), X (recurrent laryngeal).
  • The 3rd arch has one muscle (stylopharyngeus). The 4th has cricothyroid. The 6th has every other intrinsic laryngeal muscle.
  • The 1st arch is Meckel's cartilage. The 2nd arch is Reichert's cartilage.
  • Stapedius is the smallest muscle in the body and belongs to the 2nd arch.

Why the recurrent laryngeal nerves loop

The recurrent laryngeal nerve belongs to the 6th arch and lies just behind that arch's artery. When the heart and great vessels move down into the chest, the nerve is pulled down with them.

On the left, the 6th arch artery stays as the ductus arteriosus (later the ligamentum arteriosum), so the left nerve hooks under the aortic arch. On the right, the lower part of the 6th arch artery disappears, so the right nerve hooks under the right subclavian artery, a 4th arch derivative.

Pouches and what they become

The pouches are outpocketings of the endoderm lining the pharynx. Each pouch sits just below its arch, so pouch 1 lies between arches 1 and 2.

Pouch derivatives
PouchBecomes
1stMiddle ear cavity, auditory (Eustachian) tube, mastoid antrum
2ndCrypts of the palatine tonsil, tonsillar fossa
3rdDorsal wing: inferior parathyroid gland. Ventral wing: thymus
4thDorsal wing: superior parathyroid gland. Ventral wing: ultimobranchial body, which gives the parafollicular C cells

The inferior parathyroid starts higher than the superior one but ends up lower. It travels down with the thymus and passes the 4th pouch tissue on the way. For the same reason, an ectopic inferior gland is often found near the thymus.

Mnemonic: "Every Teacher Teaches In School Carefully."
Ear (1st pouch) · Tonsil (2nd) · Thymus (3rd, ventral) · Inferior parathyroid (3rd, dorsal) · Superior parathyroid (4th, dorsal) · C cells (4th, ventral)

Grooves and membranes

The 1st groove deepens to form the external acoustic meatus. The 1st membrane becomes the tympanic membrane, with ectoderm outside, endoderm of the 1st pouch inside and mesenchyme between.

The 2nd arch grows down over grooves 2 to 4 and buries them in a space called the cervical sinus. The sinus normally closes. If any part stays open, it leaves a branchial cyst, sinus or fistula.

Clinical correlation: branchial cyst and fistula

A painless swelling along the anterior border of sternocleidomastoid in a child or young adult is a branchial cyst until proven otherwise. Most come from the 2nd arch region.

A 2nd arch fistula opens on the skin at the lower third of the anterior border of sternocleidomastoid. Its inner opening is in the tonsillar fossa, and its tract passes between the internal and external carotid arteries.

Neural crest cells and syndromes

Neural crest cells reach the arches from the hindbrain. Rhombomeres 1 and 2 supply the 1st arch, rhombomere 4 the 2nd arch, rhombomeres 6 and 7 the 3rd arch, and rhombomere 8 the 4th and 6th arches. Rhombomeres 3 and 5 send few cells because most of theirs die by apoptosis.

These cells shape the face skeleton, the connective tissue of the thymus and parathyroids, and the outflow tract of the heart. When they fail, several organs are affected at once.

Clinical correlation: DiGeorge syndrome (22q11.2 deletion)

The most common microdeletion syndrome (about 1 in 4000 births). The key gene is TBX1, which drives arch development. The 3rd and 4th pouches develop poorly.

  • Thymic hypoplasia, which causes T-cell immunodeficiency.
  • Parathyroid hypoplasia, which causes hypocalcaemia, tetany and seizures.
  • Conotruncal heart defects: tetralogy of Fallot, truncus arteriosus, VSD.
  • Cleft palate, abnormal face and low-set ears.

CATCH-22: Cardiac defects, Abnormal face, Thymic hypoplasia, Cleft palate, Hypocalcaemia, chromosome 22.

Two other 1st arch problems are worth knowing. Treacher Collins syndrome (TCOF1) comes from too few neural crest cells in the 1st arch and gives small cheekbones, a small jaw and ear defects. Pierre Robin sequence gives a small mandible, a tongue that falls back, and cleft palate.

Quick revision in 5 steps

  1. Count the arches1, 2, 3, 4 and 6. The 5th is absent.
  2. Say the nervesV, VII, IX, X (SL), X (RL).
  3. One muscle each3rd: stylopharyngeus. 4th: cricothyroid. 6th: all other laryngeal muscles.
  4. Map the pouches1 ear, 2 tonsil, 3 thymus and inferior parathyroid, 4 superior parathyroid and C cells.
  5. Link the syndrome3rd and 4th pouch failure is DiGeorge: CATCH-22.

Viva corner

Why does the left recurrent laryngeal nerve loop under the aortic arch?

The nerve belongs to the 6th arch and stays behind that arch's artery. On the left, the 6th arch artery persists as the ductus arteriosus. As the heart descends, the nerve is dragged down and ends up hooked under the aortic arch and the ligamentum arteriosum.

On the right, the lower 6th arch artery is lost, so the nerve hooks under the right subclavian artery instead.

Name the derivatives of Meckel's and Reichert's cartilage.

Meckel's cartilage (1st arch): malleus, incus, sphenomandibular ligament. The mandible forms by membrane bone around it.

Reichert's cartilage (2nd arch): stapes, styloid process, stylohyoid ligament, lesser horn and upper body of the hyoid.

A child has a swelling along the anterior border of sternocleidomastoid. What is the embryological cause?

Part of the cervical sinus stayed open. The 2nd arch normally overgrows grooves 2 to 4 and the space closes. A remnant becomes a branchial cyst, and a tract to the skin and pharynx becomes a fistula.

Most are 2nd arch lesions, so the internal opening of a fistula lies in the tonsillar fossa.

Practice quiz

0 of 5 answered
Question 1 of 5 Which muscle develops from the 3rd pharyngeal arch?

Stylopharyngeus is the only muscle of the 3rd arch. It is supplied by the glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX).

The other options belong to other arches: stapedius to the 2nd (CN VII), cricothyroid to the 4th (superior laryngeal nerve) and tensor tympani to the 1st (CN V).

Question 2 of 5 The superior parathyroid glands arise from which part of the pharyngeal pouches?

The dorsal wing of the 4th pouch forms the superior parathyroid. Its ventral wing forms the ultimobranchial body, whose cells become the parafollicular C cells of the thyroid.

The 3rd pouch gives the inferior parathyroid (dorsal wing) and the thymus (ventral wing). The inferior gland ends up lower because it travels down with the thymus.

Question 3 of 5 Which intrinsic laryngeal muscle comes from the 4th arch and is not supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve?

Cricothyroid is the only intrinsic laryngeal muscle supplied by the superior laryngeal nerve (its external branch). It is a 4th arch muscle.

Every other intrinsic laryngeal muscle comes from the 6th arch and uses the recurrent laryngeal nerve. Exams often phrase this as "all except cricothyroid".

Question 4 of 5 A newborn has seizures from low calcium, no thymic shadow on the chest X-ray and truncus arteriosus. Which structures failed to develop normally?

This is DiGeorge syndrome (22q11.2 deletion, TBX1). The 3rd pouch gives the thymus and the 3rd and 4th pouches give the parathyroids, so their failure causes T-cell deficiency and hypocalcaemia.

Neural crest cells also shape the outflow tract of the heart. That is why conotruncal defects such as truncus arteriosus and tetralogy of Fallot travel with this syndrome.

Question 5 of 5 Which structure is derived from Reichert's cartilage?

Reichert's cartilage (2nd arch) forms the stapes, styloid process, stylohyoid ligament, and the lesser horn and upper body of the hyoid.

The malleus, incus and sphenomandibular ligament come from Meckel's cartilage of the 1st arch. The stapes is the only ear ossicle from the 2nd arch.
